## Mechanism

ETF daily log returns exhibit **excess kurtosis** relative to a Gaussian
null — fatter tails than a normal distribution would produce.

The candidate economic mechanism: daily returns are not generated by a
single, constant-variance process. Volatility itself is time-varying and
clusters (calm periods followed by turbulent ones), and returns occasionally
reflect discrete shocks (macro surprises, earnings-adjacent moves in
constituent holdings, liquidity events) rather than smooth diffusion. A
mixture of a low-volatility regime and a high-volatility regime — or,
equivalently, a return-generating process with occasional large jumps —
produces a distribution with a sharper peak and fatter tails than a normal
distribution with the same variance, i.e. positive excess kurtosis. This is
one of the most consistently replicated stylized facts in empirical finance
across asset classes and time periods.
